Three Flipper Counties

Robeson County in North Carolina, Cambria County in Pennsylvania and Macomb County in Michigan all voted for Barack Obama, then for Donald Trump three times. This page shows what each county did in the November 3, 2026 election and how that compares with the past.

Every number here was checked again against an official results page, a local newspaper, or a reference table. Each section links to where the figures came from. Figures we could not confirm were taken out, and the corrections are listed near the end.

Key words

Flipper county
A county that voted for one party's presidential candidate and then switched to the other party.
Margin
How far ahead the winner was, in percentage points. R +5 means the Republican got 5 points more than the Democrat. D +5 means the Democrat did.
Midterm
An election held between presidential elections (2018, 2022, 2026). The top race is usually governor or U.S. Senate.
Split ticket
Voting for a Democrat in one race and a Republican in another on the same ballot.
Unofficial / official
Results are unofficial until the county or state certifies them. Small changes can still happen before that.
Recount and protest
A recount counts the ballots again. A protest asks election officials to review a specific problem, such as votes cast in the wrong district.

North Carolina

Robeson County: the biggest swing

Trump won Robeson by 27.6 points in 2024. Two years later the Republican U.S. Senate candidate, Michael Whatley, won the county by just 128 votes. Democrat Roy Cooper won the Senate seat statewide with 58.8%. On the same ballot, Robeson voters re-elected their Democratic sheriff with 74.6%.

What stands out

  • An 18-point move in four years. In the 2022 Senate race, Republican Ted Budd won Robeson by 18.5 points (58.2% to 39.7%). In 2026 the Republican margin was 0.4 points. 2022 data
  • Democratic turnout grew. The Democratic Senate candidate got about 35% more votes than in 2022 (10,736 to 14,488). The Republican got about 7% fewer (15,732 to 14,616).
  • Two Republican commissioners lost. Both had switched from Democrat to Republican in 2025. A third Republican seat (District 6) is down by 3 votes and under protest. Border Belt Independent
  • Party labels on the voter rolls are a weak guide. By January 2026, unaffiliated voters (39.35%) had passed Democrats (39.22%) as the largest group. Republicans were 21.11%. The Robesonian

Pennsylvania

Cambria County: still solidly Republican

Cambria was the first of the three to leave the Democrats, voting for Mitt Romney in 2012. In 2026 Republican Stacy Garrity won the county for governor with 55.8%, even though Democrat Josh Shapiro was re-elected statewide. The local paper's verdict was that the area stayed politically unchanged. The one big exception is Democrat Frank Burns, who won a 10th term in the state House.

What stands out

  • A small move toward Democrats. In 2022 Republican Doug Mastriano won Cambria by 17.0 points (32,347 to 22,795). In 2026 the Republican margin was 13.4 points, a shift of 3.6. 2022 county results
  • Registration now matches the vote. As of November 16, 2026, 54.0% of registered voters were Republicans and 34.2% Democrats, a Republican lead of 17,106. PollCheck, from Pa. Department of State data
  • Democrats last won Cambria for governor in 2014, when Tom Wolf took 54.6%. Wikipedia, 2014 governor by county
  • Two Republican state representatives had no opponent (Districts 71 and 73).

Michigan

Macomb County: a split ticket, again

Macomb voters picked Democrat Jocelyn Benson for governor and Republican Mike Rogers for U.S. Senate on the same ballot. Benson won statewide with 54.6%, and Democrat Abdul El-Sayed won the Senate seat statewide even though he lost Macomb. This is a familiar pattern: Macomb backed the Democrat for governor in 2018, 2022 and 2026, and Trump in 2016, 2020 and 2024.

What stands out

  • The governor's race barely moved. Whitmer won Macomb by 5.2 points in 2022 (51.8% to 46.6%). Benson won it by 3.6 in 2026. Wikipedia, 2022 governor by county
  • The Senate race moved a lot. Republican Mike Rogers won Macomb by 9.7 points in 2024 and by 1.8 points in 2026. Fox 2 Detroit
  • Many voters mixed parties. About 39% of people who voted did not cast a straight Democratic or Republican ballot.
  • Down-ballot statewide races were nearly tied in Macomb: about 1,000 votes for secretary of state and under 800 for attorney general.

What changed from the original explorer

This page is based on the earlier “Three Flipper Counties Explorer.” Checking it against the sources found these problems, which are fixed or removed here:

  • Robeson commissioners: the original said Republicans lost three of six seats. Only four seats were on the ballot, and two Republicans lost. A third is 3 votes behind and under protest, so it is not decided.
  • Cambria 2008: Obama won 49.2% to 48.5%, not 49.4% to 48.7%.
  • Cambria registration: the 54.0% / 34.2% figures are dated November 16, 2026, not October.
  • Mentzer quote: the original shortened it into a sentence she did not say. It now uses her actual words.
  • Removed because no source confirmed them: Robeson turnout (38.8% vs 53.8% statewide), Robeson 2016 and November 2026 registration shares, Cambria's 2016 registration lead, the “Trump +31” label on Burns's district, Macomb's 2024 straight-party numbers, the reader letter quote, and explanations about water contamination, the commission chairman and Lumbee federal recognition.

Summary of the overall results

In short: all three counties pulled back from their 2024 Trump highs in 2026. Robeson moved a long way and nearly flipped. Cambria moved a little and stayed firmly Republican. Macomb did what it usually does in midterms, backing a Democrat for governor while still leaning Republican in other races.

  1. All three are three-time Trump counties.Trump's margin was larger in 2024 than in 2016 in each: Robeson R +4.3 to R +27.6, Cambria R +36.8 to R +39.6, Macomb R +11.5 to R +13.7.
  2. 2026 was a pullback, not a full reversal.Republicans still won the top race in Robeson (by 128 votes) and Cambria (by 13.4 points). Macomb split: Democrat for governor, Republican for Senate.
  3. Compared with the 2022 midterm, only Robeson moved sharply.Robeson moved 18.1 points toward Democrats. Cambria moved 3.6 points toward Democrats. Macomb moved 1.6 points toward Republicans in the governor's race.
  4. Voters split tickets for local Democrats they know.Robeson's sheriff got 74.6%, Cambria's Frank Burns got 58.4%, and Macomb's county executive Mark Hackel got about 61%, all well ahead of their party's top candidate.
  5. Turnout explains part of Robeson's swing.Democratic Senate votes rose about 35% from 2022, while Republican votes fell about 7%.
  6. Some results are not final.Still open As of November 19, 2026, no outcome had been reported for the State Senate District 24 recount or the Robeson commission District 6 protest. Cambria's results are still marked unofficial. Macomb's are official.
